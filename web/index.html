The problem

The water comes down faster than the warning.

In Colombia, flash floods kill people every rainy season. They rise in minutes on small mountain streams, and almost none of those streams has a sensor. Official bulletins cover a whole basin or town, not the stream behind your house. Where a sensor does exist, someone often still has to go and look at the water before the alarm sounds.

~4,200 streams in the Medellín valley alone
< 30 of them with a water-level sensor

How it works

A cheap loop that never sleeps. A mind that wakes when it matters.

Measuring runs every few seconds and costs almost nothing. The language models only wake up when something changes — so one node can be watched 24/7 on a small budget.

  1. Edge

    Camera node

    Sends a frame every few seconds. A phone, an IP camera or the virtual camera in the twin.

  2. Always on

    SAM 3.1

    Segments the water against the gauge: level in centimetres and rate of rise.

  3. On events

    Muse Spark

    When something changes, it reads the scene: muddy water, logs, people near the bank.

  4. On events

    Alert agent

    Muse Spark Open remembers the last hours — trend, rain, alerts sent, who answered — and decides.

  5. Community

    WhatsApp alert

    Short, plain words, what to do now. Also web push on the dashboard.

Measure

It measures the water. It doesn't guess.

SAM 3.1 finds the water in every frame and the server reads where it meets the gauge. Calibrated once — Muse Spark reads the numbers printed on the gauge itself — every frame becomes a level in centimetres and a rate of rise.

  • Calibrates by reading the gauge, no tape measure
  • Rate of rise over the last 5 and 10 minutes
  • In the twin, every reading is checked against the true level

Decide and warn

An agent that cannot lie about the water.

The language model chooses the alert level and explains why. It never writes the numbers: alert texts come from fixed templates filled with measured values, and a verifier rejects any figure that does not match the measurements. When it is unsure, it asks a person on the ground to confirm.

  • Memory over hours: trend, rain, alerts sent, who acknowledged
  • Every number checked before a message leaves
  • Tokens and cost logged for every alert

Before sending

  • 25 cm in 10 min = measured
  • Mud, logs = scene reading
  • Level only goes up while rising
  • Text from template evacuate

Alerts on WhatsApp

The warning reaches the phone while there is still time.

No app to install. People near the stream sign up with one WhatsApp message, and Vigiax writes back the moment the water crosses a line: what is happening, in measured numbers, and what to do. One tap answers Got it, or flags a false alarm so the community can correct the station.

  • Sign up with one message, no app and no account
  • Plain words: what is happening and what to do now
  • Each level reaches the right people: a leader, the village group, everyone

Low connectivity

Built for places without signal.

The mountains that flood are the ones with the worst coverage. The node measures locally and can sound a siren with no internet. It sends numbers, not video, and keeps them until the link comes back. A node upstream warns the village downstream.

  • Local siren, even offline
  • Store-and-forward when the link drops
  • LoRa and SMS as fallback

FAQ

Questions people ask first.

Does it need special hardware?

No. Any camera that can send a photo works: a phone in a browser, an IP camera, or a small board with a camera. In the field it needs a reference — a painted gauge or a fixed mark — and, for the night, an infrared camera.

What happens at night?

A normal camera sees little in the dark, and that is the known weak spot of camera gauges. The field node uses an infrared camera or a light; the twin has a night mode to show the difference honestly.

What if the internet goes down?

The node keeps measuring locally and can sound a siren on its own. Readings are stored and sent when the link returns, and LoRa or SMS can carry the alert when there is no data at all.

Does the AI write the alert?

It chooses the level and explains why, but it never writes the numbers. Messages come from fixed templates filled with measured values, and a verifier checks every figure before anything is sent.

How much does it cost to run?

Segmentation runs every few seconds and costs a fraction of a cent per frame. The language models only run on events, and every alert logs its tokens and cost, so the bill per stream is known, not guessed.

Can I use it for my stream?

The code is open source under the MIT license. Try the twin first, then read the repository on GitHub to run a node with your own camera.
